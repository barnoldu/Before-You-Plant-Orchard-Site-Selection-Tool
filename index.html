FIELD GUIDE

Learn as you look.

This tool follows the latest supplied Commercial Fruit Site Evaluation Field Worksheet. It organizes evidence for a grower–Extension conversation before investment.

Field observations come first

Use the guided field walk to visit the high point, middle, low point, downhill outlet, and any concern spot. At each stop, record your phone location, take a photo, and add a note. Walk the whole proposed block, including high, middle, and low areas. Record where, when, and how you observed a condition. If a map conflicts with the field, document the conflict and give direct field evidence priority.

What maps can and cannot tell you

Soil maps suggest conditions to verify. Slope describes steepness; aspect describes the direction a slope faces. Relative height needs a named reference. Terrain can suggest cold-air routes, but it does not confirm airflow, frost risk, or actual hours of sunlight.

Aspect and observed shade are different. Record warming, drying, and exposure questions alongside the mapped direction; do not assign a separate aspect score.

How the rating works

Nine items contribute to three 100-point sections. A total out of 300 appears only when every item has a category, evidence source, and note. Unknowns stay blank. A high score never overrides a failed major check or a flagged field observation. The quick check, photo checklist, soil profile, row direction, weather context, and grower decision are included in the assessment.

275–300: Excellent candidate. 225–274: Good candidate. 170–224: Use caution. Under 170: High concern. These are worksheet ratings, not an investment approval.

Prepare for a useful field visit

Bring a phone or camera, notebook, depth scale, and a probe or auger if available. Take wide views and close-ups. Photograph shade at different times and wet areas after rain. Label each photo with location, direction, date, and what it shows.

Keep and share your work

Your assessment stays in this browser; it is not sent to Extension. Download a backup to preserve it. Use the report’s print option to save a PDF, then share it and your questions with your county agent or fruit specialist.

Photos, pins, and imported geometries are included in backups. The report contains a coordinate sketch and a feature list. Export GeoJSON to reuse your map in GIS. Basemap tiles are requested online; automatic soil and terrain analysis and shared accounts are not part of this first version.
